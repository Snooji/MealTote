Za'atar Manakish with Labneh, Cucumber & Tomato

BreakfastLebanese & Levantine35 minServes 4530 kcal17 g protein

Photo: Unknown · CC BY-SA 2.0 · source

Lebanon's favorite bakery breakfast: thin, blistered flatbreads slicked with a thyme-sumac-sesame za'atar paste, eaten folded around cool labneh, cucumber, tomato and mint.

Ingredients

  • 1 lb pizza dough, at room temperature
  • ¼ cup za'atar
  • 1 cup plain Greek yogurt (for quick labneh)
  • 1 English cucumber, sliced
  • 2 Roma tomatoes, cut in wedges
  • a handful of mint leaves
  • ½ cup olives

From your pantry: ⅓ cup olive oil, 1 tbsp flour, salt.

Instructions

  1. Put a baking steel, pizza stone or upside-down heavy baking sheet on the lowest oven rack and heat the oven to its highest setting (500 to 550°F) for at least 30 minutes.
  2. Stir the za'atar with the olive oil and a pinch of salt into a loose, spoonable paste. Stir the yogurt with a pinch of salt and a drizzle of oil; that is your quick labneh.
  3. Divide the dough into 4 balls. On a floured counter, roll each into a thin 8- to 9-inch round and prick it all over with a fork so it bakes flat.
  4. Spread a quarter of the za'atar paste over each round, leaving a thin bare rim, and press dimples into it with your fingertips so the oil pools.
  5. Slide one round at a time onto the hot steel and bake until the edges are golden and lightly charred in spots, 3 to 5 minutes.
  6. Serve hot, cut in quarters or folded in half, with the labneh, cucumber, tomatoes, mint and olives to tuck inside.

Tip: A thick, deep green-gray za'atar with plenty of sesame (Lebanese or Palestinian brands) tastes far better than the dusty supermarket kind. Real labneh is yogurt strained overnight; Greek yogurt is a fast stand-in.
