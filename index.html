A free meal planner that writes your grocery list for you

I got tired of meal planning apps that put the useful parts behind a subscription, so I built MealTote. Tell it who you're cooking for and how you like to eat. It plans the week, sizes everyone's portions and writes one grocery list for the stores you already shop at.

Free, no ads, no account needed. 584 recipes from 50 cuisines, priced at 36 grocery chains.

What happens when you start

  1. You answer a few questions. Who's eating and how many calories each person needs, protein and carb targets if you track them, foods you love or skip, allergies, how long you can cook and roughly what you want to spend.
  2. MealTote plans 3, 5 or 7 days. Breakfast, lunch and dinner from 584 recipes, with leftovers worked in if you want them. Don't like something? Swap it and see how calories and protein change before you do.
  3. You shop once. One list, sorted by store and aisle, split between Walmart, Aldi, Costco, Kroger, Trader Joe's, Target, Publix, H-E-B and the rest, whichever way costs less or means fewer stops.

What's in it

  • Portions for each person. One recipe can feed a 1,700-calorie day and a 2,700-calorie day. Everyone's serving is worked out separately.
  • Macros, if you count them. Set protein, carbs and fat and the planner picks dishes that fit, then tells you what to add when a meal comes up short.
  • Prices before you shop. Estimates from 36 US chains, nationwide and regional, so you know roughly what the week costs.
  • A prep plan. The chopping, sauces and grains for the week grouped into one or two sessions, so weeknights are mostly reheating.
  • A pantry that remembers dates. Keep track of what's in the fridge and what's about to go bad.
  • Your own recipe book. Save the recipes you like and add your family's own.
  • A cookbook to print. The week's plan, recipes and shopping list as a PDF to print, text or email.
  • Nothing to pay for. No premium tier. Sign in with Google only if you want your plans on more than one device.

Questions people ask

Is MealTote really free?

Yes. Every feature is free with no ads and no premium tier. MealTote runs on small optional donations.

Do I need an account?

No. Your plan saves in your browser. Sign in with Google only if you want your plans, pantry and recipe book on every device.

Which grocery stores does it support?

36 US chains, including Walmart, Aldi, Costco, Kroger, Target, Trader Joe's, Publix, H-E-B, Safeway, Albertsons, Whole Foods, Sam's Club, Meijer, Wegmans and regional grocers. Pick the ones near you and MealTote splits the list between them.

Can it build a high-protein meal plan?

Yes. Set a protein target, such as 190 g a day, and the planner favors high-protein recipes, sizes each portion to your macros, and suggests extra protein or a side when a meal comes up short.

Can I plan for my whole family?

Yes. Add everyone you cook for with their own calorie target. Recipes are cooked once and portioned per person, and the grocery list buys enough for everyone.

How accurate are the prices and nutrition?

Prices are estimates of typical shelf prices, and nutrition is calculated from each recipe's ingredients. Both are close enough to plan and budget with; check labels and shelf prices for exact numbers.

It takes about a minute, and you can change anything afterward.
