# MealTote design rules

MealTote is a free meal planner made by one person for real households. It should feel like a useful kitchen tool someone cared about,
not a startup template. When in doubt, choose the plainer, more specific option.

## Look

- **Warm and food-first.** Off-white or warm dark backgrounds, greys with no blue in them. No navy or indigo backgrounds, no purple, no neon,
  no rainbow gradients. Themes change style and accent, but every one passes WCAG AA in light and dark.
- **Text reads left-aligned.** Headings can sit with their text; body copy, lists and FAQs are left-aligned. Don't center long paragraphs.
- **Lists over walls of cards.** One row per thing with the action on the right, details on tap. Use a card only when it holds something
  you act on as a unit (a meal slot, a recipe in the library, a store's shopping list). No grids of identical feature boxes.
- **Photos support the text.** Lists use small thumbnails beside the name (`photos/t/`, about 64×48). Big photos belong on the recipe itself
  and in the recipe library. A recipe without a good photo shows the MealTote logo, never a weak photo.
- **Status is text and outline, never a filled card.** Expired, over budget, missing at your stores: say it in a colored label or border.
  Small chips (like calorie changes) can carry a light tint; whole rows and cards never do.
- **Real type.** The theme's display face for headings and big numbers, a plain sans for everything else, monospace only for numbers that line up.
- **Motion is short and rare.** A sheet slides up, a toast appears. Nothing loops, nothing bounces. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.

## Words

- **Write like a person.** First person where it's about why MealTote exists ("I built it because..."), plain second person everywhere else.
  No stock phrases: "Everything you need to...", "Supercharge", "Seamless", "Unlock", "Revolutionize", "In today's busy world".
- **No symbol-icons standing in for words.** Buttons say Close, Save, Support, Add to list. No ♥ ✓ ✕ → in labels.
  The tab bar's line icons are fine because every one has a word under it.
- **Say what a number means** next to it: "kcal per serving", "g protein", "per person, per meal", "today".
- **Be specific.** "584 recipes priced at 36 chains" beats "tons of recipes". Don't round up or invent numbers, ratings or testimonials.
- **Explain empty states** with the one action that fills them.

## Behaviour

- **Phones first.** 40 px touch targets, the tab bar pinned to the bottom, details in a bottom sheet, 16 px form fields (iOS doesn't zoom).
- **Nothing jumps.** Tapping an option never scrolls the page; long lists load as you scroll.
- **Every action can be undone or confirmed.** Deletes take a second tap; removals say what happened.
- **Accessible.** WCAG AA contrast, visible focus, a label on every input, alt text on content photos.

## Search pages

Generated by `tools/export-cookbook.mjs` (recipes, cuisines, meal plans, sitemap). One H1 per page, real descriptions, schema.org data that
matches what's visible on the page. Never add review stars, ratings or counts that aren't real.
