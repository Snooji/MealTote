# Swedish Meatballs with Cream Gravy, Mash & Lingonberries

**Dinner** · Scandinavian · 55 min · serves 5 · skill 2/3

> Köttbullar the way Swedes love them: soft, nutmeg-and-allspice beef and pork meatballs in a creamy brown gravy, with buttery mashed potatoes, tart lingonberry jam and quick-pickled cucumber.

## Ingredients

- 10 oz ground beef
- 10 oz ground pork
- 2 slices bread, crusts removed, diced
- 1 egg
- 1 small onion, grated
- 5 tbsp butter, divided
- 2 cups beef broth
- ½ cup heavy cream
- 2½ lb russet potatoes, peeled and chunked
- ½ cup milk
- lingonberry jam, to serve
- 1 English cucumber, very thinly sliced

From the pantry: 1 tbsp oil, 3 tbsp flour, ¼ tsp nutmeg, ¼ tsp allspice, 3 tbsp white vinegar, 1 tbsp sugar, salt, pepper

## Steps

1. Toss the cucumber with the vinegar, sugar, 3 tbsp water and a pinch of salt and set aside. Boil the potatoes until tender, then mash with 2 tbsp butter, the milk and salt.
2. Mix the grated onion and its juice with the bread until soggy. Add the beef, pork, egg, nutmeg, allspice, ¾ tsp salt and pepper and mix well. Roll into about 28 small balls.
3. Heat the oil in a large skillet over medium-high and brown the meatballs in two batches, about 3 minutes each; they don't need to be cooked through. Set aside and pour off excess fat.
4. Lower the heat to medium, melt the remaining butter, stir in the flour for 1 minute, then gradually whisk in the broth until smooth.
5. Return the meatballs and simmer briskly, stirring now and then, until the gravy thickens and the meatballs are cooked through, 8 to 10 minutes. Stir in the cream and simmer 2 minutes.
6. Serve over the mash with gravy, a spoon of lingonberry jam and the drained pickled cucumber.

**Tip:** Grating the onion rather than chopping keeps the meatballs extra tender and smooth. No lingonberries? Whole-berry cranberry sauce is a very close match.

Adapted from [RecipeTin Eats](https://www.recipetineats.com/swedish-meatballs/).

## Storing

Fridge 4 days · freezer up to 3 months. Freeze meatballs in gravy. Reheat gently on the stove with a splash of broth; microwave the mash with a little milk.
