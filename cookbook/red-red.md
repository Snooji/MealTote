# Ghanaian Red-Red with Sweet Fried Plantains

**Lunch** · Nigerian & West African · 45 min · serves 4 · skill 1/3

> Black-eyed peas simmered in a gingery, chili-warmed tomato sauce that is red twice over, with golden fried plantains on the side. Hearty, cheap and vegan.

## Ingredients

- 2 cans (15.5 oz) black-eyed peas, rinsed
- 1 can (14.5 oz) diced tomatoes
- 2 Roma tomatoes, chopped
- 1 onion, thinly sliced
- 1-inch piece ginger, grated
- 2 garlic cloves, grated
- 1 jalapeño (or ½ Scotch bonnet), minced
- 2 ripe plantains

From the pantry: ¼ cup oil, oil for frying, 2 tbsp tomato paste, 2 tsp smoked paprika, 1 tsp curry powder, ½ tsp red pepper flakes, salt, pepper

## Steps

1. Warm ¼ cup oil with the smoked paprika in a medium pot over medium heat for 1 minute so it turns brick red. Add the onion and cook until soft and starting to brown, 6 to 8 minutes.
2. Stir in the ginger, garlic, jalapeño, curry powder and pepper flakes for 1 minute, then the tomato paste for 1 minute more.
3. Add the canned and fresh tomatoes with a pinch of salt and simmer, stirring now and then, until thick and jammy, about 12 minutes.
4. Fold in the black-eyed peas with ½ cup water and simmer 10 minutes so they soak up the sauce. Mash a few against the pot to thicken it, then season with salt and pepper.
5. Meanwhile peel the plantains, slice on the diagonal and shallow-fry in ¼ inch of oil over medium-high until caramelized, 2 to 3 minutes per side. Serve with the beans.

**Tip:** Red palm oil is traditional and gives the dish its color and earthy flavor; look for it at African markets, or keep using the paprika oil trick. A sprinkle of gari on top adds crunch.

Adapted from [Zoe Adjonyoh via loveFOOD](https://www.lovefood.com/recipes/71106/red-red-stew-recipe).

## Storing

Fridge 5 days · freezer up to 3 months. The beans keep and freeze well; fry plantains fresh. Reheat beans on the stove over medium with a splash of water, 5 minutes.
